Can two different mistakes cost the same?

At one fixed input, the deterministic target is 10. Treat four predictions as equally weighted repeated fits. Choose a set, then inspect where its squared error comes from.

Interpret this result:

Hint and scope

Mean squared error averages squared distances from 10. Squared bias is the squared distance of the mean from 10; variance averages squared distances from that mean, dividing by 4, not 3. These empirical quantities satisfy MSE = squared bias + variance over the chosen sample. Irreducible noise is absent in this deterministic toy.

This is not a generalization proof or a population estimate. No complexity control is modeled: increasing complexity does not always lower bias or raise variance.

Optional transfer: What happens for [10, 10, 10, 10]? Which error components disappear?
